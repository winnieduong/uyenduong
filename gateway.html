Experience Showcase

“What? So What? Now What?”

UC Vietnamese International Student Association (VISA) — Hoi Khoe Phu Dong, Fall 2025

What?

This year, I served as Program Coordinator for the University of Cincinnati Vietnamese International Student Association (VISA). Just one month before our largest event of the semester—a sports day inspired by traditional Vietnamese games—our executive board went through restructuring, and several members stepped down. Suddenly, our team was smaller, roles were unclear, and deadlines were approaching quickly. As coordinator, I had to reorganize responsibilities, simplify our event plan, and maintain morale. We focused on clear communication, realistic timelines, and highlighting the cultural meaning behind each game. Despite the challenges, the event exceeded expectations. Many students RSVP’d, turnout was high, and participants actively engaged with the traditional activities.

So What?

I learned that leadership is less about control and more about adaptability. I saw how quickly plans can change, and how important it is to stay calm, prioritize what matters most, and support the people doing the work. I also realized that cultural programming is powerful when it creates connection—students were not just attending for fun, but to feel included and to experience Vietnamese traditions in a welcoming space. This strengthened my confidence in navigating uncertainty and reminded me that meaningful events depend on both solid logistics and a clear purpose.

Now What?

In the next year, I expect to face new academic and leadership challenges where timelines, teams, and expectations may shift. I will apply these lessons by planning with flexibility, setting up communication and roles early, and building backup options for key responsibilities. Most importantly, I will keep anchoring my work in mission—why the project matters—so that when obstacles come up, the team can stay aligned and motivated.

Global Citizen Scholar

This experience helped shape my definition of a Global Citizen Scholar as someone who bridges cultures, leads with resilience, and creates inclusive spaces where diverse identities are valued. Being “global” is not only about traveling or studying abroad—it is about listening, representing communities thoughtfully, and building connections across differences through action.
